LinkedIn Headline + Bio Generator

Your headline is the first thing anyone sees. Enter your role, your field and one real win, and get three headlines plus an About section you can paste straight into LinkedIn.

Learn: how to use this well

  1. Headline = who you help + what changes. "Web Developer" says nothing. "Web Developer | Helping small businesses get customers online" says everything.
  2. Lead with proof, not adjectives. "Results-driven" is a claim. "Built 12 business websites" is evidence. Evidence wins.
  3. Write the About section like a short story. What you do, one proof, what you are looking for. Three short paragraphs, no life story.
  4. Use the words recruiters search. If jobs say "React developer", your profile should say "React developer" — not just "front-end wizard".
  5. End with an invitation. "Open to conversations about..." tells the reader what to do next.
  6. Update it when you change. A headline from two jobs ago quietly costs you opportunities every week.

Pro tips

  • Test headlines by asking a friend: "What do I do, from this line alone?" If they hesitate, rewrite.
  • Keep the headline under 120 characters so it is never cut off on mobile.
  • Match your headline to your resume — a mismatch makes both look careless.

Frequently asked questions

Should I put my job title or what I do?

What you do, for whom. Titles differ between companies; outcomes are understood everywhere.

How long should the About section be?

Three short paragraphs is the sweet spot: what you do, one proof, what you want next. Longer than that rarely gets read.

Can I use this if I am a student or fresher?

Yes. Use your field of study and one project or result instead of a job win — "Built a class scheduling app used by 200 students" is a real win.

Is my information private?

Yes. Everything is generated in your browser and never sent anywhere.
