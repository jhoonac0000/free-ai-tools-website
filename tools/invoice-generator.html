Learn: how to use this well

  1. Invoice the day the work is done. Every day of delay is a day later you get paid. Same-day invoices are a habit, not a chore.
  2. One invoice, one job. Mixing jobs on one invoice creates questions. Separate invoices get paid faster.
  3. Describe the work plainly. "Homepage design — 3 sections, mobile-friendly" beats "web services". Clients pay what they understand.
  4. Number every invoice. INV-001, INV-002 — it looks professional and makes your own records easy at tax time.
  5. State payment terms on the invoice. Even a simple "Due within 14 days" prevents most awkward follow-ups.
  6. Follow up once, politely. A short reminder 3 days after the due date collects more money than an angry message ever will.

Pro tips

  • Keep a copy of every invoice in one folder — your future accountant will thank you.
  • If a client always pays late, add the cost of waiting into your next quote instead of arguing.
  • For repeat clients, save a template invoice and change only the items and date.

Frequently asked questions

Is this invoice legally valid?

An invoice is a payment request, not a legal document by itself. For tax purposes, keep your records and check your local rules for what an invoice must show (business name, date, amount are the usual basics).

Can I add my logo?

This free version keeps it clean and text-based so it prints perfectly everywhere. A premium template pack with logo support is planned for the store.

Where is my invoice saved?

Nowhere on our side — it exists only on this page until you print or save it as a PDF. Save that PDF in your own records folder.

What if the client asks for a different currency?

Write the currency in the price fields (for example "250 USD") and the totals will follow the same unit. Currency symbols are kept as typed.
