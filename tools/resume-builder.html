Your cover letter

Learn: how to use this well

  1. One page if you can. Hiring managers scan resumes in seconds. Cut anything that does not help this specific role.
  2. Put the role in the summary. Your first two lines should say what you do and what you are good at — not your life story.
  3. Use numbers. "Handled 40 customers a day" beats "responsible for customers". Numbers make claims believable.
  4. Match the job ad's words. If the ad says "customer support", use those exact words — many companies filter by keywords first.
  5. Tailor the cover letter. One paragraph about why this company, one about your best proof, one polite close. Never send the same letter everywhere.
  6. Proofread twice. One typo can end an application. Read it out loud once before sending.

Pro tips

  • Save your resume as a PDF with a clear file name: YourName-Role.pdf.
  • For a niche role (nurses, fresh graduates), add one line of niche proof at the top of the summary.
  • Keep a master resume with everything, then delete lines to make each one-page version.

Frequently asked questions

Is this resume builder really free?

Yes. There is no account, no payment and no watermark. Your resume is built in your browser and you print or save it yourself.

Where does my information go?

Nowhere. Everything you type stays on your device. We have no server receiving your resume — see our Privacy page.

Should my resume be one page or two?

One page for most jobs under 10 years of experience. Two pages only when the extra page adds real proof, not filler.

Can I use this for a specific niche, like nursing?

Yes. Put the niche in your target role and add one line of niche proof (licence, ward experience, patient numbers) in the summary.
