Image to PDF — Photos into One PDF

Turn phone photos and scans into one clean PDF — the format every portal and office actually wants. Your file never leaves your device — everything happens in your browser, nothing is uploaded to any server.

Learn: how to use this well

  1. Photograph documents like a scanner. Flat on the table, camera directly above, daylight from the side, all four corners visible. A good photo makes a good PDF page; no converter fixes a skewed, shadowed shot.
  2. Order is the document. Add pages in reading order — form, then ID, then certificates. The PDF follows your list exactly.
  3. One topic per PDF. “Ahmed-Certificates.pdf” beats one giant file of everything. Offices and portals file what they can identify.
  4. Check the size. Phone photos are heavy. If the PDF passes a portal's MB limit, compress the photos with the Image Compressor first, then rebuild.

Pro tips

  • Each image becomes a page sized to the image itself — nothing is stretched or squeezed.
  • For text documents, portrait photos make portrait pages; mixed orientations are kept as photographed.
  • Open the finished PDF once before sending. You are looking for the page you would be embarrassed by.

Frequently asked questions

Are my images uploaded anywhere?

No. The PDF is built in your browser on your device. Nothing is uploaded or stored.

How many images can I add?

As many as your device handles comfortably. For very long sets on older phones, build the PDF in two parts and merge them with the PDF Merge tool.

Will the PDF be huge?

It carries your photos at their original quality, so it can be. Compress the images first if a portal's limit is tight.

Can I rearrange the order?

The PDF follows the order you added the images. Remove the set (reload the page) and add them again in the right order if needed.
