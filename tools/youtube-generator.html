YouTube Title + Description + Hashtag Generator

One keyword in, ten titles, a description and hashtags out. Built from proven title frameworks in your browser — no AI server is involved.

Learn: how to use this well

  1. Promise one thing. A title that promises everything promises nothing. Pick the single reason to click.
  2. Curiosity + clarity. "I Tried X for 30 Days" works because it is curious AND clear. Pure curiosity ("You won't believe this") feels like clickbait.
  3. Put the keyword early. Viewers and search both read the first words first.
  4. Match title and video. If the video does not deliver what the title promised, viewers leave early — and that hurts the video more than a boring title would.
  5. Write the description for search, not just humans. First two lines should say what the video teaches, in plain words, with the keyword in them.
  6. Hashtags: a few, relevant. Five to ten real tags beat thirty random ones.

Pro tips

  • Generate ten titles, then pick by asking: "Would I click this if I did not know the creator?"
  • Test titles by changing one thing at a time — the hook, the number, or the audience.
  • Your thumbnail and title are one unit: they should add information, not repeat each other.

Frequently asked questions

Will these titles work for Shorts too?

Short titles work well for Shorts, but Shorts rely more on the first second of video than on the title. Use a short, punchy title and put the hook in the video itself.

How many hashtags should I use?

YouTube allows up to 15, but 5–10 relevant ones is the practical range. Irrelevant tags do not help and can look spammy.

Is clickbait okay if the video is good?

No — a misleading title earns one click and loses a subscriber. Curiosity is fine; a promise the video breaks is not.

Can I use this for other platforms?

Yes. The title frameworks work for blog posts and social videos too — just shorten them for platforms that cut titles off.
