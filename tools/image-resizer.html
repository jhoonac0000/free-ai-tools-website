Image Resizer — Pixels or Percentage

Make a picture exactly the size a website, portal or marketplace demands. Your file never leaves your device — everything happens in your browser, nothing is uploaded to any server.

Learn: how to use this well

  1. Keep the aspect ratio locked. Changing width and height independently stretches faces and logos. Unless a portal demands exact pixels for both, let one side follow the other.
  2. Resize down, not up. Making an image smaller keeps it sharp. Enlarging invents pixels that were never photographed and looks soft — if a portal wants 1000 px and your photo is 400 px, re-take or re-scan the photo instead.
  3. Know the common numbers. Passport-style portals often want 350 × 450 px or similar; marketplaces favour 1000–2000 px squares; profile pictures are usually displayed under 400 px.
  4. Dimensions are not file size. A 350 × 450 photo can still exceed a KB limit. Resize for pixels here, then run the Image Compressor if the portal also caps KB.

Pro tips

  • Resize a copy, keep the original full-size — you cannot un-shrink a photo.
  • Batch consistency makes listings look professional: same pixel size for every product photo.
  • If a form says “2 × 2 inch photo”, at 300 DPI that is 600 × 600 px.

Frequently asked questions

Is my image uploaded anywhere?

No. Resizing happens in your browser on your device.

Why does my resized image look stretched?

The aspect ratio was unlocked and both sides were changed independently. Re-check “Keep aspect ratio” and set only the width — the height will follow.

Can I make an image bigger?

You can set larger dimensions, but the tool cannot invent detail — the result will look softer. A bigger original is always the better fix.

What is the difference between this and the Compressor?

This tool changes pixel dimensions. The Compressor changes file size (KB) to hit a target. Portals often demand both — resize first, then compress.
