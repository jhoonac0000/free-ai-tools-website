Background Remover — Transparent PNG

Cut the subject out of a photo and download it on transparency. An AI model runs on your own device — your photo is never uploaded. The first time you use it, the model (a few tens of MB) downloads once; after that it is cached by your browser.

Learn: how to use this well

  1. Contrast is everything. A subject that clearly stands out from its background — different colour, different brightness — cuts out cleanly. A white shirt against a white wall is the hardest case for any remover.
  2. Good light beats good software. Even, shadow-free light keeps edges honest. Harsh shadows become part of the “subject” or part of the background in confusing ways.
  3. Hair is the final exam. Wispy hair and fur are where free removers differ from paid ones. Zoom into the edges of the result before you use it anywhere public.
  4. Use the cut-out on purpose. Transparent PNGs belong on product listings, thumbnails, ID-style photos on coloured backgrounds, and design work — places where the rectangular photo edge would look wrong.

Pro tips

  • For marketplace products, place the cut-out on pure white — that is the look buyers trust and most platforms require.
  • If the edges look rough, shrink the image slightly in its final design; small sizes hide small flaws.
  • Run the tool on the largest original you have, then resize the result — never the other way round.

Frequently asked questions

Is my photo uploaded to a server?

No — and this one is different from most “AI” removers: the model itself downloads to your browser once, and your photo is processed on your own device. Nothing is uploaded.

Why is the first run slow?

The AI model (a few tens of MB) downloads once and is then cached by your browser. Later runs on the same device start immediately and are much faster.

Why is there a checkerboard behind my preview?

That pattern is how editors show transparency. The downloaded PNG really is transparent — the checkerboard is only in the preview.

The edges look imperfect. Can it be better?

Hair, glass and same-colour backgrounds are genuinely hard. A clearer, better-lit photo with a plainer background gives this (and every) remover a much easier job.
