Cold Email + Subject Line Generator

One specific observation, one offer, one soft question — that is the whole formula. This generator builds it from frameworks in your browser; no AI server is involved.

Learn: how to use this well

  1. Keep it under 90 words. Long cold emails do not get read. One observation, one offer, one question — then stop.
  2. Make the first line about them. "I noticed your clinic..." beats "We are a leading agency..." every time.
  3. One offer, not a menu. Listing five services makes the reader choose — and choosing is work, so they close the email.
  4. End with a yes-or-no question. "Worth a quick look?" is easier to answer than "Let me know a good time for a 30-minute call next week."
  5. Follow up, politely, twice. Most replies come from follow-ups, not the first email. Add something new each time; never just write "bumping this up".
  6. Never buy email lists or blast hundreds. Small, researched batches protect your sender reputation — and your conscience.

Pro tips

  • Send from a real name and a real domain, not a free address, once you can.
  • If they reply "not interested", thank them and stop. That list is closed.
  • Track replies, not opens. An open means nothing; a reply means a conversation.

Frequently asked questions

How many cold emails should I send per day?

Start small — a handful of researched emails beats a hundred generic ones. Small batches also protect your email account from spam filters.

Should I include a link in the first email?

One link at most, and only if it proves something (a sample, a scan, a relevant page). Links in a first email can trigger spam filters, so keep it to one.

What if nobody replies?

Check three things: is the observation specific, is the offer one clear thing, and did you follow up? Most cold campaigns fail on one of those three, not on effort.

Is cold email spam?

Not when it is researched, relevant, sent to a business address, and stops on request. Bulk, bought-list blasting is spam — and it damages your domain.
