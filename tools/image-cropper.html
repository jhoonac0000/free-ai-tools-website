Image Cropper — Drag & Crop

Cut away what does not belong and keep what matters. Drag the box, pull the corner, download. Your file never leaves your device — everything happens in your browser, nothing is uploaded to any server.

Learn: how to use this well

  1. Crop for the subject, not the scene. Most photos improve by removing the outer third. Faces and products should fill the frame the way they fill your attention.
  2. Match the ratio to the destination. Squares for marketplace grids and profile pictures, 16:9 for video thumbnails and banners, 3:4 for ID-style and passport photos. Cropping to the right ratio stops platforms from doing it badly for you.
  3. Leave a little breathing room. Subjects touching the frame edge look accidental. A small margin reads as deliberate.
  4. Crop before you compress or resize. Order matters: framing first, then pixel size, then file size. Each step keeps the quality the next one needs.

Pro tips

  • For ID-style photos, head and shoulders centred, eyes near the upper third — that is the framing officials expect.
  • Straighten mentally: a horizon that tilts 3 degrees distracts more than any other flaw. Re-shoot if the tilt is strong.
  • The download is at full original resolution of the selected area — what you select is exactly what you get.

Frequently asked questions

Is my image uploaded anywhere?

No. Cropping runs in your browser; the image never leaves your device.

How precise is the crop?

The box maps exactly to the original image's pixels, and the tool reports the crop size in real pixels before you download.

Can I type exact numbers instead of dragging?

Dragging with the aspect presets covers most jobs. For exact pixel dimensions afterwards, pass the crop through the Image Resizer.

Does cropping reduce quality?

The selected area keeps its original pixels and quality. Cropping only discards what is outside the box.
